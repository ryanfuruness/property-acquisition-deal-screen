# Dubai income-producing residential building: underwriting conventions (research as of 21 Nov 2026)

Legend: **[V]** verified against the cited page during this research; **[S]** secondary source only (portal or broker blog), indicative; **UNVERIFIED** means market practice I could not confirm with a source.

## 0. Market regime in late 2026 (this affects every assumption)
- Rents are falling. In Q2 2026 Dubai average residential rents fell **6.2% QoQ and 2.6% YoY** (CBRE, via [Gulf News, 29 Jul 2026](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-rents-ease-62-while-home-prices-stay-above-2025-levels-1.500623468)). In Q3 2026 YoY apartment rents fell **12.8% in Dubai Marina, 12.2% in Business Bay and 10.4% in JVC**, but only **0.4% in International City Ph 2/3** (Savills, via [Khaleej Times, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/rents-in-dubai-fall-over-12-dubai-marina-business-bay-and-jvc-lead)). [V]
- New-lease volumes fell 20% in the six months to July 2026. The average new tenancy was AED 60k (−6% YoY) and renewals averaged AED 65k, flat. Landlords are giving concessions ([AGBI, Jul 2026](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/real-estate/2026/07/uae-rental-market-splits-as-dubai-cools-and-the-north-heats-up/), citing DXB Interact/DLD data). [V]
- Supply: about 160–170k units are scheduled for 2026, roughly 85–88% of them apartments, against a long-run delivery rate of about 36k a year ([Knight Frank Q4 2025](https://www.knightfrank.ae/newsroom/article/2026/2/dubai-residential-market-review-q4-2025); Savills via [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/02/25/dubai-rents-to-stop-rising-by-end-of-year-after-half-decade-boom)). Scheduled supply far exceeds what is actually delivered. [V]
- Abu Dhabi, not Dubai, froze rent increases at 0% on 2 Jun 2026 ([The National](https://thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/06/02/abu-dhabi-freezes-rents-for-residential-commercial-and-industrial-properties/); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/industry/rent-freeze-in-dubai-relief-for-tenants-amid-west-asia-turmoil), whose headline wrongly says "Dubai"). I found **no Dubai rent freeze**. The "3-year freeze" articles date from a 2021 *draft* Dubai law that was never enacted ([C&W Core, 2021](https://cushwake.ae/en/news/dubai-landlords-try-to-get-higher-rents-on-new-tenancy-contracts-before-3-year-freeze-takes-effect)). [V]

## 1. Leasing
- **Ejari:** registration of every tenancy is mandatory, and an unregistered contract "lacks legal standing" ([Property Finder](https://propertyfinder.ae/blog/paying-rent-in-dubai)). Registration costs about AED 178 online or AED 220 at a trustee centre [S] ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/how-much-does-a-property-manager-cost/)). The rent roll should carry an Ejari number per unit, and DLD's open data includes Ejari contracts (section 7).
- **Lease length:** 12 months, renewing annually, is the norm. **UNVERIFIED** as a statistic, but Law 26/2007 builds its renewal and notice mechanics around expiry.
- **Payment:** post-dated cheques (PDCs) are still common, and "many landlords still prefer one to four cheques per year" ([Property Finder](https://propertyfinder.ae/blog/paying-rent-in-dubai)). Twelve cheques or monthly payment is possible. On 23 Jun 2026 DLD launched **Flexi Rent**, which offers monthly, quarterly or semi-annual instalments through 11 partner landlords and managers, including Wasl and Deyaar ([DLD](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/news-media/dubai-land-department-launches-flexi-rent-initiative-and-strengthens-strategic-partnerships-to-advance-rental-market-accessibility/)). Model implication: more cheques means more collection risk and a smaller deposit/PDC cushion.
- **Bounced cheques:** these were largely **decriminalised from 2 Jan 2022**. A returned cheque is now an *executive instrument*, so the holder can seek direct execution, and banks must pay partially where funds exist ([Betterhomes](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/betterinformed/bounced-cheque-law-in-uae-the-changes-landlords-and-tenants-need-to-know)). [S] Eviction for non-payment requires a 30-day notice via notary or registered mail (Law 33/2008, Art. 25(1)) ([DLP](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2009/Law%20No.%20%2833%29%20of%202008%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2826%29%20of%202007.html)). [V]
- **Security deposit:** about **5% of annual rent unfurnished and 10% furnished**. This is market convention; the law sets no percentage ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/housing/renting-a-home-in-dubai-how-much-is-the-security-deposit-1.1716906865349)). Deposits are liabilities that transfer to the buyer and should be credited at closing. **UNVERIFIED** as a written rule, but standard practice.
- **Rent increases:** these are governed by **Decree 43 of 2013**. The current rent is compared with the RERA index "average market rent". Rent ≤10% below the index allows **0%**; 11–20% below allows **5%**; 21–30% below **10%**; 31–40% below **15%**; >40% below **20%** ([Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/rent-increase-rules-dubai/)). Increases are allowed only at renewal. Since **2 Jan 2025** the benchmark is the AI-based **Smart Rental Index**, which classifies each building by structure, finishes, maintenance, location and services ([DLD](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/news-media/smart-rental-index-announcement); [Dubai Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/january/02-01/smart-rental)). The DLD page cites "Decree No. 34 of 2013", which appears to be a typo for 43. [V] DLD has clarified that the 90-day notice still applies under the new index ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/rents-in-dubai-landlords-must-give-90-day-notice-to-increase-rates-under-new-rental-index)). The bands cap *increases* only. Nothing forces a decrease, but a tenant can leave at expiry.
- **Notices (Law 26/2007 as amended by Law 33/2008)** ([DLP](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2009/Law%20No.%20%2833%29%20of%202008%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20%2826%29%20of%202007.html)) [V]:
  - Art. 14: any change of terms at renewal, including rent, requires notice **≥90 days before expiry**, unless the parties agreed otherwise.
  - Art. 25(2): at expiry the landlord can evict only for demolition or reconstruction, major renovation (with a DM report), own or first-degree-relative use, or **sale**. This requires **12 months' notice via notary public or registered mail**. A landlord who reclaims a unit for own use cannot re-let it for 2 years ([Khaleej Times](https://khaleejtimes.com/uae/legal/dubai-owners-cannot-rent-out-apartment-for-2-years-after-vacating-it-for-personal-use)). [S]
- **Mark-to-market implications for the engine:**
  1. *Under-rented units* cannot be reset to market on renewal. Model the uplift as `min(market − current, band% × current)` per renewal, with each step re-tested against the index. Full reversion happens only on tenant turnover, which you cannot force; the 12-month eviction route is for sale, own use or renovation, not for repricing.
  2. *Over-rented units* are the 2026 risk: in-place rents signed in 2024–25 may sit above today's market, and tenants can leave or negotiate at expiry. Haircut to market at each expiry and apply downtime.
  3. Any rent change at the next expiry needs a 90-day notice, so the first ~3 months of renewals after closing are already fixed.
  4. Use the RERA index rent (unit-level, from the DLD calculator) and not the OM's "market rent".

## 2. Acquisition costs

The legal split is 2/2, but market practice is that **the buyer pays all 4%** | [Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/dld-fees-dubai/) [S]; [REC Dubai, Jul 2026](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/legal/dld-service-fees-2026-trustee-office-noc-mortgage-registration-every-charge-explained) [S] |

| Broker commission | **~2% + 5% VAT**, typically buyer-paid in secondary sales, and negotiable on large deals | [Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/dld-fees-dubai/) [S] |
| Mortgage registration | **0.25% of the loan + ~AED 290** | same [S] |
| Valuation | AED 2.5–3.5k for a unit. A building RICS valuation is much higher (**UNVERIFIED**) | same |
| Share deals | DLD treats a change in shareholding of a property-owning company as a registrable transfer, so **4% still applies**. Intra-group relief is discretionary | [REC Dubai](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/investment/buying-dubai-property-through-company-2026-spv-offshore-structures) [S]. Confirm with counsel |
| Fees financed? | Banks no longer finance fees, so they are paid in cash | [Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/dld-fees-dubai/) [S] |

DLD has stated that registration fees were not increased ([DLD](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/news-media/dubai-land-department-no-increase-in-real-estate-registration-fees)). A reasonable all-in for a building is **≈6.1–6.5% plus mortgage registration** (my inference).

**VAT** (FTA guide VATGRE1; [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/uae-real-estate-vat-tax-authority-interpretation); [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/middle-east/en/services/tax/perspectives/uae-fta-publishes-vat-public-clarification-updated-guidance-re-sector.html)) [V]:
- Residential, first supply (sale or lease) within 3 years of completion: **zero-rated**.
- Other residential sales and leases: **exempt**.
- Commercial (shops and offices in a mixed-use building), including serviced or hotel apartments: **5%**.
- A change of use does not alter the VAT on the previous sale, but it changes how the next sale is treated.

Model implications: residential rent is exempt, so **input VAT on opex and capex is largely irrecoverable** and should be modelled gross. This is my inference from the exempt-supply rules. Ground-floor retail rent carries 5% output VAT, so apportion input VAT in a mixed-use building.

## 3. Operating costs (whole-building owner)
- **Strata vs single title:** Law 6 of 2019 (Jointly Owned Property) governs buildings divided into units with owners' associations and Mollak-approved service charges ([DLP](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2019/Law%20No.%20(6)%20of%202019%20Concerning%20Ownership%20of%20Jointly%20Owned%20Real%20Property%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html)). For a **single-title building there is no OA or service charge**, and the owner pays the actual costs: FM, security, cleaning, MEP, lifts, insurance and common-area utilities. If the building is strata-titled and you buy every unit, you still pay the OA budget. Ask which applies.
- **Benchmarks, unit level (OA service charge, AED/sq ft/yr)** [S]: JVC ~12 (9–16); Business Bay ~18 (12–52); DSO 9–13; International City ~8 (6.5–11); Arjan/Al Barsha South ~12.5 ([REC Dubai](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/property-management/dubai-service-charges-explained-what-owners-pay-2026)). Official building-level figures come from the **DLD/RERA Service Charge Index** via Mollak and the Dubai REST app. A single-owner low-rise G+4 building can be far cheaper: one Liwan 2 listing shows AED 15k per year for the whole 20-unit building ([dubizzle](https://uae.dubizzle.com/property-for-sale/residential/residential-building/2026/4/22/brand-new-building-fully-tenanted-fixed-2-283111/)). That is a broker figure; treat it with suspicion.
- **District cooling:** Empower charges a capacity (demand) charge of **AED 750/RT/yr** plus consumption of **AED 0.568 (56.8 fils) per RTh**; Emicool consumption is **AED 0.56 (56 fils) per RTh** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/housing/uae-what-are-the-district-cooling-charges-in-dubai-1.1720017124527)). [S] Who pays depends on the lease ([Bayut](https://bayut.com/mybayut/chiller-free-district-cooling-dubai)) [S]:
  - In "chiller-free" buildings the landlord bears all cooling, both capacity and consumption.
  - Otherwise the tenant pays the cooling provider directly.
  - Where a building has its own central chiller plant (not district cooling), the plant's DEWA power and maintenance are landlord opex (**UNVERIFIED** as a norm; check the T-12).
  - Flag OMs that advertise "chiller-free" without a cooling line in opex.
- **DEWA:** in-unit electricity and water are paid by the tenant. The landlord pays common areas.
- **Municipality housing fee:** **5% of annual Ejari rent**, billed monthly on the DEWA bill and **paid by the tenant**. UAE nationals are exempt; owner-occupiers pay 5% of the RERA-assessed rent ([Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/dubai-municipality-housing-fee/)). [S] Confirmed as **not a landlord cost**.
- **Property management:** **5–8% of gross rent** for long-term residential (7–10% commercial). Leasing fees are about one month's rent per new tenant (5–8%), and RERA does not cap these fees ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/how-much-does-a-property-manager-cost/)). [S] Institutional whole-building mandates are often 3–5%. **UNVERIFIED**
- **Insurance, maintenance, sinking fund:** I found no authoritative Dubai benchmark. **UNVERIFIED** suggested defaults: building insurance ~0.05–0.15% of reinstatement value; R&M 3–5% of gross rent, rising with age; capex reserve AED 1–3/sq ft/yr.
- **Vacancy and collection:** Dubai Residential REIT reported **98.9% occupancy** in Q1 2026 ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/business/real-estate/dubai-residential-reit-reports-48-revenue-growth-989-occupancy-in-q1-lxgryhlq)), a best-in-class institutional benchmark. Given falling rents and heavy supply, a screening default of **5–8% vacancy plus 1–2% bad debt** is my **UNVERIFIED** suggestion, along with **1–2 months downtime per turnover**.

## 4. Tax (what the model needs)
- **Corporate tax:** **0% on taxable income up to AED 375,000 and 9% above** ([u.ae](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/finance-and-investment/taxation/corporate-tax); [MoF](https://mof.gov.ae/en/news/ministry-of-finance-confirms-applicable-taxable-income-threshold-for-corporate-tax-abu-dhabi-uae/)). Rental net income is taxed for UAE companies and for **non-resident juridical persons** that own UAE immovable property, which creates nexus under Cabinet Decision 56/2023 ([Khaleej Times](https://khaleejtimes.com/business/corporate/uae-corporate-tax-ministry-clarifies-tax-on-real-estate-immovable-property)). [V]
- **Natural persons:** real estate investment income is **outside CT** if no licence is held or required (Cabinet Decision 49/2023; FTA natural-persons RE guide, Oct 2024). Ejari is not a licence, but a DET holiday-home permit is ([Hadef](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/federal-tax-authority-releases-guide-on-real-estate-investment-for-natural-persons-issued-october-2024/)). [V]
- **QIFs and REITs:** **Cabinet Decision 34/2025** applies to periods from 1 Jan 2025.
  - If a QIF's UAE immovable property share exceeds 10%, juridical investors are taxed on **80% of their pro-rated real estate income** unless ≥80% is distributed within 9 months.
  - REIT conditions: real estate assets above AED 100m; ≥20% free float, or 2+ unrelated institutional investors holding ≥20%; average real estate ratio ≥70%, now based on income-producing property ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/ae/en/home/insights/2025/05/updates-on-qifs-reits-qlps-and-non-resident-nexus-rules.html)). [V]
- **Withholding tax:** 0%. **No property tax** beyond the housing fee, which tenants pay. **VAT**: see section 2.
- **Engine:** offer a toggle between pre-tax and after-tax at 9% on (NOI − interest − depreciation), with a AED 375k band. Treat the vehicle type (individual / UAE company / QIF / REIT) as an input.

## 5. Market metrics
- **Yields:** the widely quoted citywide residential average is **6–8%**, with **JVC, Business Bay and Dubai South gross 7–9%** and Marina **net ~5.5–6.5%** ([Khaleej Times, early 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubai-yields-stay-among-worlds-highest-as-gcc-property-surge-rolls-into-2026)). Those figures are unattributed. A DLD-derived dataset gives an average gross yield of **8.16%** across 5,528 buildings, licensed CC BY 4.0 ([Mendeley, Jul 2026](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/rwnn99mdz5/1)).
- **Unit-level apartment price and ROI, H1 2026** ([dubizzle](https://www.dubizzle.com/blog/property/dubai-h1-sales-market-report-2026/)) [S]:

| Area | Avg AED/sq ft (unit) | Gross ROI |
|---|---|---|
| International City | 1,116 | 7.10% |
| Dubai Silicon Oasis | 1,339 | 6.66% |
| JVC | 1,497 | 7.00% |
| Business Bay | 2,408 | 5.54% |
| Al Barsha | not covered | — |

- **Whole buildings trade at a discount to the sum of unit values and are usually quoted on BUA.** Examples:
  - **Al Barsha 1**: 48 apartments, 3 offices and 2 shops; AED 65m on 110,000 sq ft BUA ≈ **AED 591/sq ft BUA** ([dubizzle](https://dubai.dubizzle.com/property-for-sale/residential/residential-building/2026/4/23/whole-building-rented-premium-location-2-325364/)).
  - **Liwan 2**: G+4, 20 apartments; AED 29m on 27,158 sq ft BUA ≈ **AED 1,068/sq ft BUA**, marketed as "fixed return" ([dubizzle](https://uae.dubizzle.com/property-for-sale/residential/residential-building/2026/4/22/brand-new-building-fully-tenanted-fixed-2-283111/)).
  - These are asking prices, not trades. There is no institutional whole-building cap-rate survey I could verify. **UNVERIFIED**: institutional net initial yields of roughly 6.5–8.5% for mid-market buildings.
- **Quoting conventions:** brokers almost always quote **gross yield = in-place (or potential) annual rent ÷ asking price**, with no costs deducted. Institutions use **NIY = NOI ÷ (price × (1 + ~6.5% costs))**. The engine should compute all three and label them. **UNVERIFIED** as a written convention, but standard.
- **Price trend:** Knight Frank forecasts ~1% mainstream and ~3% prime growth for 2026 ([KF](https://www.knightfrank.ae/newsroom/article/2026/2/dubai-residential-market-review-q4-2025)). Prices were +1.9% YoY in Q2 2026 (CBRE via Gulf News, above).
- **Ownership eligibility:** foreign buyers can own only in designated freehold areas (Regulation 3 of 2006 and later lists; [DLP](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2006/Regulation%20No.%20(3)%20of%202006.html)). Many whole-building plots in older areas, possibly including parts of Al Barsha, are non-freehold and restricted to UAE/GCC owners. **UNVERIFIED for specific areas**; the engine must flag this as a check.
- **Financing:**
  - UAE Central Bank base rate is **3.90%** after a **+25 bp hike in Sep 2026**, held in Oct. Experts expect no cuts before 2028 ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae-borrowers-prepare-for-higher-loan-costs-into-2028); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2026/09/17/uae-interest-rates-set-to-rise-but-did-borrowing-get-easier-when-they-were-low/)).
  - **3M EIBOR ≈ 4.30%** at the 29 Oct 2026 fixing [S] ([Westgate](https://westgatedubai.com/eibor-rate-mortgage-dubai-2026/)). The CBUAE fixing page is Cloudflare-blocked, so re-check it at [centralbank.ae](https://centralbank.ae/umbraco/Surface/Eibor/GetEiborData).
  - Bank retail product terms: Emirates Islamic offers **up to 70% LTV, up to 15 years, completed commercial or residential-complex property** ([EI](https://www.emiratesislamic.ae/en/finance/commercial-property-finance)); RAKBANK up to 75% LTV ([RAKBANK](https://www.rakbank.ae/en/business/business-finance/business-finance/real-estate-finance)); ADCB up to 10 years ([ADCB](https://www.adcb.com/en/islamic/business/products-solutions/financing-solutions/commercial-banking-financing-solutions/commercial-property-finance)).
  - **UNVERIFIED** institutional term sheets for an income-producing building: LTV 50–60%; **3M EIBOR + 2.0–3.0%**; 10–15-year amortising, sometimes with a balloon; **DSCR covenant 1.20–1.40x**; assignment of rents and an escrow or collection account.

## 6. Documents and common misstatements
- **Broker OM (typical):** location and plot number; G+N floor count; plot area, BUA and NSA; unit mix; parking; title type (freehold or non-freehold, single title or strata); completion year and DM completion certificate; occupancy; "annual rental income"; "ROI %"; asking price; photos. The OM rarely includes a real opex budget. **UNVERIFIED** as a formal standard.
- **Rent roll columns:** unit no.; type (studio/1BR/2BR/shop); area (sq ft); tenant (individual or company); Ejari no.; start and end dates; annual rent; no. of cheques and cheque dates; deposit; chiller arrangement; status (occupied, vacant, notice served, under renewal, legal case). DLD Ejari fields such as `contract_start_date`, `annual_amount`, `actual_area` and `tenant_type` are listed on [Dubai Pulse](https://www.dubaipulse.gov.ae/data/dld-registration/dld_rent_contracts-open) and can be used to reconcile.
- **Misstatements to detect:**
  1. "ROI" or "yield" that is gross and computed on asking price.
  2. **Potential** rent, where vacant units are included at asking rent.
  3. Fixed-return or **bulk/master lease** to a related operator: covenant risk, rent possibly above market, and the 12-month notice needed to end it.
  4. Rents above the RERA index or the current market (2026 over-renting).
  5. Ignoring chiller (chiller-free), common DEWA, FM, insurance and management costs.
  6. Mixing sq m (title deeds) with sq ft (listings): 1 m² = 10.7639 ft².
  7. **BUA vs NSA/saleable area**: AED/sq ft on BUA looks cheap, and efficiency is often 75–85% (**UNVERIFIED**).
  8. Annualising a single month, or counting rent-free months as income.
  9. Counting commercial units' VAT-inclusive rent.
  10. Ignoring the 4% DLD fee in the yield.
  11. Expired Ejari or holding-over tenants counted as occupied.
  12. Deposits not shown as liabilities.

## 7. Public data: what is downloadable
- **DLD Real Estate Data** (https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/open-data/real-estate-data): Transactions, Rents (Ejari), Projects, Valuations, Land, Building, Unit, Broker and Developer, each with **"Download as CSV"** (also Excel/PDF). It requires a mandatory date range (DD-MM-YYYY) and **Google reCAPTCHA** (`captchaEnabled: true`), and earlier years are redirected to data.dubai. Tested: the page loads (HTTP 200). Its JavaScript calls `https://gateway.dubailand.gov.ae/open-data/{transactions|rents|units|buildings|…}` and `/export/csv`. I did **not** call the API because of the captcha, so programmatic bulk download did **not work** without a browser. **Licence:** none stated; the page says "© Dubai Land Department – All Rights Reserved" with a link to the T&Cs. Treat it as restricted and check the T&Cs.
- **Dubai Pulse** (https://www.dubaipulse.gov.ae/data/dld-transactions/dld_transactions-open and https://www.dubaipulse.gov.ae/data/dld-registration/dld_rent_contracts-open):
  - Metadata pages describe CSV, updated daily, but the metadata itself was last updated in Apr/May 2024. The licence is "notspecified".
  - Fields: transactions include `actual_worth`, `meter_sale_price`, `procedure_area` and `building_name_en`; rents include `annual_amount`, `actual_area` and `contract_start/end_date`.
  - The API needs an API key and secret (Bearer token).
  - Tested: WebFetch could read the metadata, but `curl` from this sandbox got **TLS connection reset** and **no file was obtained**.
- **data.dubai**: HTTP 200, but it returned "Request Rejected" (WAF), so it failed from this sandbox.
- **Rental index:** the DLD Smart Rental Index calculator is unit-level and interactive. I found no bulk download. **UNVERIFIED** whether a bulk export exists.
- **Alternatives:** the CC BY 4.0 Mendeley DLD-derived yields dataset (above). For a portfolio demo, ship a **synthetic or sample CSV** that follows the Dubai Pulse schema, and add an adapter for real exports downloaded manually.
